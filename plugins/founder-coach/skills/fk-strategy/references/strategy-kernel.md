# The strategy kernel

From Richard Rumelt's book *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy* (2011). Rumelt's point is that a
strategy is not a goal or a vision. It is a short chain of reasoning with three parts, which
he calls the **kernel**. This skill uses the kernel as the spine of `strategy.md`, adapted for a
bootstrapping founder with little money and little time.

## The three parts

### 1. Diagnosis: what is the real challenge?

A plain statement of what makes this hard, right now, for this founder. It names the obstacle,
not the ambition. A good diagnosis simplifies: out of everything going on, it picks the one or
two things that matter most.

For a bootstrapper the challenge is usually some mix of: too little time, no audience yet, a
problem that may not be painful enough, customers who are hard to reach, or a crowded
alternative (often a spreadsheet or doing nothing).

- Weak: "We want to help freelance photographers with their finances."
- Better: "Freelance photographers already put up with a spreadsheet and a once-a-year panic.
  The pain is real for the ones who chase late client payments, but Priya has 15 hours a week
  and no way yet to reach photographers outside one meet-up group."

### 2. Guiding policy: the overall approach

The broad choice about how to deal with the diagnosis. It rules things out. A guiding policy
that rules nothing out is not one.

- Weak: "Build the best bookkeeping app for creatives."
- Better: "Win the photographers who chase late payments first, by selling done-for-you
  payment matching through the meet-up group before building any software."

### 3. Coherent actions: what we will actually do

A handful of actions (three to five) that carry out the policy and **support each other**.
They use the founder's real hours, skills and reach. Each one should make the others easier;
actions that pull in different directions are a sign the policy is not really chosen.

- "Run monthly payment matching by hand for five paying photographers at a fixed fee."
- "Write down the same steps for each, so the repeated ones can be automated later."
- "Share what we learn in the meet-up group's chat every fortnight to grow the audience."

## Bad strategy: what the review looks for

Rumelt names four hallmarks of bad strategy. The critical review
(`review-checklist.md`) checks every draft and update against them.

| Hallmark | What it looks like in a founder's strategy | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluff** | Big words with little meaning: "leverage AI to empower creators", "a holistic platform" | Ask what it means in a sentence a customer would say. Rewrite in plain words or cut it |
| **Failure to face the challenge** | No diagnosis, or one that ignores the evidence (interviews where nobody raised the pain; an offer test nobody paid for) | Name the evidence that was skipped and ask what the real obstacle is |
| **Mistaking goals for strategy** | "Be the #1 platform for X", "reach £10k a month by June", "grow fast" | A goal says where you want to be, not how you will get there against the obstacle. Keep it as a goal, then ask for the diagnosis and policy |
| **Bad strategic objectives** | A long list of things to do with no order, or objectives as hard as the original problem ("get lots of customers") | Pick the one or two that unlock the rest; make each something the founder can do this month |

When you flag bad strategy, be kind and specific: quote the line, say which hallmark it is,
and offer a better version or the question that would get to one. A goal is fine to keep; it
just goes alongside the strategy, not in place of it.

## Adapting the kernel for bootstrappers

- **No market sizing.** Do not estimate total market, market share, valuation or anything for
  investors. A bootstrapper's question is "where can I win with the time and money I have?",
  not "how big could this be?".
- **Evidence beats ambition.** The diagnosis should cite what real people said or did (from
  customer research, interviews, an offer test). If there is no evidence yet, say so in the
  diagnosis: "we do not yet know whether…" is a valid, honest diagnosis.
- **Small is a strength.** A narrow wedge a large company would ignore is often the best
  policy for a team of one or two.
- **The founder is part of the diagnosis.** Their hours, skills, runway and who they can
  reach (from `founder-profile.md`) shape what is possible.
