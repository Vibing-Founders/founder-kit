# Founder types and their time sinks

Five common founder types. Each brings real strengths and has classic ways of spending
time on work that feels productive but does not move the current stage forward.

Use these to watch for time sinks, not to label people. Most founders are a mix; record
the mix in the profile (for example "designer, some operator"), based on what the founder
said about themselves and how they have spent their time. Ask if unsure. When you spot a
time sink, name the behaviour kindly, say why it does not help this stage, and offer the
better move.

For bootstrappers, "chasing investors" becomes chasing anything that is not a paying
customer: awards, press, partnerships, followers.

## Engineer / hacker

**Traits:** builds late into the night, cares about architecture, can build the whole
product alone, enjoys the technical problem for its own sake.

**Time sinks:**
- refactoring or perfecting code before anyone has used it;
- designing for scale that will not arrive for years;
- adding features because they are interesting to build, not because a customer asked;
- building infrastructure (login, payments, hosting) instead of using a service;
- delaying a pre-sale because "the backend isn't ready".

**Better move:** sell first, build the least needed to deliver, use services for
everything that is not the product's reason to exist.

## Designer

**Traits:** thinks visually, cares about craft, makes products people love to use.
Many bootstrapping founders are former product or UX designers.

**Time sinks:**
- iterating on colours, typefaces and animations;
- rebranding after every piece of feedback;
- waiting for the perfect component library or design system;
- mocking up future features no customer has asked for;
- delaying a payment test because checkout "isn't pretty yet".

**Better move:** a plain page with a price and a payment button tests more than a
beautiful one without. Polish what paying customers touch, once they are paying.

## Hustler

**Traits:** talks to people easily, sells, opens doors, gets early customers and
attention.

**Time sinks:**
- polishing a pitch deck before there is a paying customer;
- taking press, partnership or networking meetings that produce no revenue;
- treating sign-ups, followers or waitlists as demand;
- promising features to close a deal, then derailing the build;
- outsourcing the build cheaply and spending weeks fixing it.

**Better move:** point the selling energy at a paid offer. Count payments, not
interest. Sell only what exists or can be delivered by hand.

## Domain expert

**Traits:** knows an industry deeply, speaks its language, has credibility and
contacts. Former nurses, accountants, teachers and tradespeople often start here.

**Time sinks:**
- market studies and reports instead of customer conversations;
- customising the product for every early customer;
- attending conferences instead of closing a first paid pilot;
- building compliance and security for big customers who have not signed;
- writing guides and whitepapers with no offer and no price.

**Better move:** use the network for conversations and a paid pilot. Build one version
for a narrow group; say no to one-off customisations.

## Operator

**Traits:** thinks in systems, processes and numbers; good at making something that
works run smoothly.

**Time sinks:**
- org charts, goals and roles for a team that does not exist;
- dashboards and reporting before the first sale;
- processes and meetings that slow a team of one or two;
- planning for scale before customers stay;
- hiring before there is revenue to pay for it.

**Better move:** save systems for stages 6 and 7. Before then, the only number that
matters is whether people pay and stay.

## Using the type in coaching

- Record the type(s) and the time sinks to watch in `founder-profile.md`.
- In a **sanity check**, check the request against that founder's list first.
- A team of two with different types is a strength: suggest splitting work so each
  founder's time sinks are checked by the other.
