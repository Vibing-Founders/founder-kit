# Personas and qualifying questions

The ICP says which customers to go after. Personas describe the different people within
that group. Negative personas describe the people who look like customers but are not.
Qualifying questions let the founder tell them apart in a first call or a sign-up form.

## ICP or persona?

- **ICP-level**: true of every good-fit customer (they invoice clients, they work
  rotating shifts). Put it in the ICP profile.
- **Persona-level**: true of some good-fit customers and not others (some hate admin and
  want it gone, others want control and check every number). Put it in the persona.
- **Pain points** always live in the ICP pain point section. A persona lists which of
  those pains hit them hardest, by name, and links to them.

## Buyer personas

Each persona has:

- **A memorable name** that captures who they are: "Deadline-Juggling Duncan",
  "The Receipt-Shoebox Freelancer", "Night-Shift Nadia". Never "Persona A", "Primary" or
  "Secondary".
- **Who they are**: role (B2B) or situation (B2C), in a sentence or two.
- **What they are trying to do** and **what drives them**.
- **Pains that hit hardest**: names of ICP pain points, not copies.
- **How they buy**: how they found similar tools, who else is involved, what they said
  about price.
- **Evidence**: which conversations this persona is based on, with one short quote.
- **Qualifying questions** that identify them.

Create a new persona only when at least one conversation shows a distinct pattern.
Flag personas based on a single conversation as provisional.

## Negative personas

People to avoid or screen out: they will not get value, will churn, will demand things
the product will never do, or cost more to serve than they pay. Each has:

- **A memorable name**: "The Hobby Shooter" (photographs friends' weddings twice a year,
  no real bookkeeping need), "The Meal-Prep Perfectionist" (wants macros tracked to the
  gram; the app is for busy people who just need to eat).
- **Warning signs**, drawn from what customers actually said or did.
- **Why they are a poor fit**.
- **Screening questions**.

Only create negative personas from evidence: a conversation that went nowhere, a
customer who clearly needs something else, a pattern of poor-fit sign-ups.

## Qualifying questions

Good qualifying questions:

- ask about past behaviour, not intentions ("How did you handle your last tax return?"
  beats "Would you use a tool for tax?");
- are answerable in one line;
- separate personas cleanly (each one's answer points to a different persona);
- come from patterns in the conversations, not guesswork.

Format:

```markdown
| Question | Good-fit answer | Poor-fit answer | Points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| "How many paid shoots did you do last month?" | 4 or more | "Just the odd one for friends" | Deadline-Juggling Duncan / The Hobby Shooter |
| "Who does your books today?" | "Me, at night, in a spreadsheet" | "My accountant handles everything" | Receipt-Shoebox Freelancer / not a fit |
```

For B2B, focus on role, company and how buying decisions are made. For B2C, focus on
situation, routines and past spending.
