# Strategic bets and kill criteria

A strategy for an early founder is a set of guesses. Writing them down as **bets** makes it
possible to tell when one has failed. `strategy.md` holds **3 to 5** strategic bets. Fewer than
three usually means the risky assumptions have not been named; more than five means nothing has
been chosen.

## The shape of a bet

Each bet has four parts:

- **Hypothesis:** what we believe, in one or two sentences, specific enough to be wrong.
- **What must be true:** the two or three assumptions that, if false, sink the bet. These are
  the load-bearing ones.
- **How we'll test it:** the smallest, cheapest test, usually a conversation, a landing page
  with a price, a pre-sale, or a manual version. Not a product, unless the bet is about the
  product.
- **Kill or pivot criteria:** the result, by a date, that means stop or change course.

Label bets `B1`, `B2`… so `journey.md` can name the one being tested now (see "Link to the
coach" below).

## Kill criteria must be testable

Each kill criterion needs at least one of: **a number**, **a date**, or **an observed
behaviour** (something people did, not said). Ideally all three.

| Not testable | Testable |
|---|---|
| "If it doesn't get traction" | "Fewer than 5 paid pre-orders at £15 by 2026-12-01" |
| "If people don't like it" | "Fewer than 3 of the next 8 interviewees mention late payments before we ask, by 2026-11-15" |
| "If it isn't working after a while" | "If fewer than 2 of 5 done-for-you customers renew for a second month" |
| "If agencies aren't interested" | "No agency agrees to a paid pilot after 10 direct conversations by 2026-11-30" |
| "If it's too much work" | "If one customer's monthly matching takes more than 3 hours by hand after the third month" |

Waitlist sign-ups, likes, followers and "I'd use that" are weak signals; prefer payments,
renewals, unprompted mentions and repeat use. If the only available measure is weak, say so
and choose the strongest one the founder can actually run.

Distinguish **kill** (stop this bet) from **pivot** (change one thing: a different group, a
different price, a different way to deliver). Say which a result triggers.

## Example bets (invented)

Two nurses, Aisha and Tom, building a meal-planning app for shift workers, 12 and 10 hours a
week:

```markdown
### B1. Night-shift nurses will pay for a weekly plan that fits their rota

- **Hypothesis:** Nurses on rotating night shifts will pay £4 a month for a meal plan built
  around next week's rota, because they already waste food and buy takeaways after nights.
- **What must be true:** they plan meals at all; the rota is known a week ahead; £4 is less
  than one wasted takeaway.
- **How we'll test it:** a landing page with a £4/month pre-order shared in two nurses' groups
  Aisha belongs to, plus five interviews about the last week of nights.
- **Kill or pivot criteria:** fewer than 10 paid pre-orders by 2026-12-15 → pivot to a
  different group (paramedics, care workers) before killing.
```

## Link to the coach

`journey.md` (written by `fk-coach`) holds the **current** bet, the one being tested now.
`strategy.md` holds the full set. When a strategic bet becomes the current bet, the coach writes
"Tests strategic bet: B1" in `journey.md`. This skill never writes that line itself; when a
change here means the current bet should change, say so and suggest a coach check-in.
