# Failure modes to watch for

Common ways bootstrapping founders lose months. Watch for them at any stage. When you see
one, name it kindly and specifically ("it sounds like the last two weeks went on the
dashboard; no customer has asked for it yet"), say why it matters now, and offer the
smallest better move. Do not lecture, and do not list every failure mode at once.

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| **Building before the problem is clear** | Features, tech choices or an app, but no one-sentence problem and customer | Go back to the finer steps in `journey.md`: problem and who has it first |
| **Building before positioning** | A product nobody can describe in a line: who it is for and why it beats their workaround | Write the one-line positioning and test it in conversations |
| **Desk research instead of conversations** | Reports, market sizes, competitor spreadsheets, no interviews | Five conversations with people who have the problem this week |
| **Opinions treated as evidence** | "Would you use…?" and "sounds great" counted as demand | Ask about the last time it happened; ask for money or a commitment |
| **Skipping the offer test** | Going from interviews straight to building | A landing page with a price, a pre-sale or a done-for-you offer first |
| **Sign-ups mistaken for demand** | A waitlist or free trial count treated as validation | Count payments and people who come back |
| **Scaling before retention** | Ads, new channels or hires while customers leave | Find out why they leave; fix the offer (often back to stage 3) |
| **Changing too many things at once** | New price, new audience and new features in the same week | One change per test, so you know what worked |
| **No kill criteria** | A bet that can only ever be "nearly there" | Agree what result by what date means stop or change course |
| **Endless polishing** | Logo, copy, design or code reworked with no customer asking | Ship the plain version to real people |
| **Customising for everyone** | Every early customer gets their own version | One narrow offer; say no to one-offs |
| **Ignoring the founder** | Plans that need 40 hours a week from someone with 15, or more runway than exists | Rebuild the plan around the real constraints in the profile |
| **Done-for-you with no exit** | Hand-delivered work growing with no written process or build trigger | Apply the guardrails in `journey.md` |
| **Chasing things that are not customers** | Awards, press, partnerships, followers, fundraising | Ask: does this get a paying customer closer? |

## Tone

- Specific beats general: quote what the founder said or did.
- Kind beats blunt: the founder is doing their best with limited time.
- Offer, do not order: "one option is…", then let them decide.
- Name progress too: spotting and dropping a time sink is worth celebrating.
