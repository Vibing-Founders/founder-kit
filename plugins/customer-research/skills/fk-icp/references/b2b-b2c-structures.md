# B2B and B2C structures

The ICP looks different depending on who pays.

## Deciding which applies

- **B2B**: the customer buys on behalf of a business, even a one-person business. Look
  for: invoices, clients, a team, "we", company tools, expenses, someone else who must
  approve the purchase, a business bank account.
- **B2C**: the customer buys for themselves or their household, with their own money,
  for personal life. Look for: home, family, personal routines, "my own money".
- **Sole traders and freelancers** sit on the line. If they pay from business income and
  the product serves their work (a bookkeeping tool for freelance photographers), treat
  it as B2B with a very small company profile. If it serves their personal life (a
  meal-planning app for shift workers), it is B2C.
- If the evidence is mixed or thin, say so, pick the closer fit, and add "confirm who
  pays and from which budget" to research gaps.

State the decision and the evidence for it in the analysis and in the ICP header.

## B2B ICP sections

- **Company profile**: industry, size (people), location, how the business makes money.
  Only what customers said; leave unknowns blank and list them as gaps.
- **Pain points**: business problems, each with quotes and `Possible Solutions`.
- **Goals**: what the business is trying to achieve.
- **Decision-making**: who uses it, who pays, who approves, how long a decision takes,
  what they compare against.
- **Budget**: how they pay for tools today and what they said about price. Report what
  was said; do not estimate revenue or lifetime value.
- **Success measures**: how they would know it worked, in their words.
- **Tools today**: software, spreadsheets and workarounds they already use.

B2B buyer personas are usually **roles** within the ideal company (the owner who pays,
the assistant who does the work, the accountant who checks it).

## B2C ICP sections

- **Customer profile**: life stage, situation, routines, location, values, only as
  stated or clearly shown in stories.
- **Pain points**: personal problems, each with quotes and `Possible Solutions`.
- **Goals**: what they are trying to achieve in their life.
- **How they decide**: how they find, compare and choose products like this.
- **Budget and price sensitivity**: what they said about spending.
- **Success measures**: how they would know it worked, in their words.
- **Digital habits**: apps, devices, where they spend time online.

B2C buyer personas are usually **situations or mindsets** within the target group
(the night-shift nurse who eats at odd hours, the parent planning for a family around a
rota).

## Changing structure later

If later evidence shows the first call was wrong (a "consumer" app is being expensed by
employers), restructure the ICP, record the change in the version log, and tell the
founder: this is a meaningful finding about who pays.
